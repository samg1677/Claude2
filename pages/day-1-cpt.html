The short version

What Day 1 CPT actually is

Curricular Practical Training is off-campus work authorization tied directly to your academic program. Normally an F-1 student completes one full academic year before becoming eligible. Federal regulation makes one exception: where a graduate program requires practical work as part of the curriculum, the school may waive that waiting period.

That's all "Day 1 CPT" means. It isn't a separate visa, a loophole, or a permit you apply to the government for. It's CPT — authorized by your school's Designated School Official, in a program built so the work is part of the degree.

  • Authorized by the school, term by term, before you start work — never retroactively.
  • Tied to one employer and one role that relates to your field of study.
  • Contingent on full-time enrollment — your classes are what keep the status alive.
  • Renewed each term, so a new offer or new employer needs new authorization.

The parts that aren't a sales pitch

Day 1 CPT works, and thousands of students use it — but it's a closely watched area of immigration practice. Students who run into trouble almost always run into one of these three. Go in knowing them.

You are a student first

Enrollment is what holds your status. Attend the on-campus weeks, submit the work, stay full-time. A program you don't actually attend is the fastest way to lose the status you enrolled to protect.

Never work before authorization

CPT is authorized in advance for a specific employer, role, and date range, and it's printed on your I-20. Working a day before the start date — or after the job changes — is unauthorized employment.

Full-time CPT can cost you OPT

Twelve months or more of full-time CPT at one degree level eliminates OPT at that level. Part-time CPT does not. Decide this deliberately at the start, with an attorney — not halfway through.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is Day 1 CPT legal?
CPT is authorized under 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(i), which lets a school waive the one-academic-year requirement when a graduate program requires immediate participation in practical training. Programs using this provision are subject to scrutiny, so what matters is that the work is genuinely integral to the curriculum, that you attend and complete your coursework, and that every term of CPT is authorized before you start working. Review your individual case with a licensed immigration attorney.
Can I keep my current job?
In most cases yes, as long as the role relates to your field of study and your employer provides an offer letter with the details the school requires. Your authorization names a specific employer, location, and date range, so each employer and each term has to be authorized by your DSO before you work.
How fast can I start?
There are six start dates a year — January, March, May, July, September, November. Once your documents are complete, admission and I-20 issuance take days rather than months, which is why students inside a grace period can often still act in time. The bottleneck is almost always document collection, so start early.
Do I have to move to California?
No. The programs are hybrid: most coursework is online, and you come to campus twice per eight-week term, in Week 1 and Week 8, at either Los Angeles or San Diego. Many students fly in for those sessions and work from where they already live.
What happens after I graduate?
If your program is STEM-designated, you become eligible for OPT plus the STEM extension — up to 36 months of post-graduation work authorization — provided you haven't used twelve or more months of full-time CPT at that degree level. That window is typically enough for two or three more H-1B lottery attempts.
Will employers accept CPT?
Most do, and many have hired CPT students before. What an employer needs from you is simple: proof of authorization on your I-20 and clear dates. We help you explain the arrangement to HR so it doesn't become a reason to pass on you.
How much does the whole thing cost?
Tuition is paid in interest-free installments roughly every two weeks over two years after a single down payment, and a $5,500 grant is available to qualifying students. An advisor gives you the exact number for your program before you commit to anything.
