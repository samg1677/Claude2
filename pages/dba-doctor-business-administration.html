Doctor of Business Administration

The doctorate built for people who are already working

While a PhD trains full-time academics, researchers, and professors who spend years on campus producing theory, a DBA takes the same doctoral rigor and points it at the real world: you research and solve actual business problems, often the ones inside the company you already work for.

The hybrid executive format brings you to the Los Angeles or San Diego campus twice every 8 weeks; the rest fits around your job. Most students finish in about three years while working full-time the entire way - and when you finish, you hold the title of Doctor.

Any accredited master's degree qualifies you. Moving up a degree level is exactly what unlocks your new period of work authorization and time in the U.S., and unlike most universities, your CPT is not capped - it runs from your first day of class straight through graduation.

How an 8-week term works

Built around a full-time job, not against it

Each term runs eight weeks. You are on campus at the beginning and the end - the rest is online, on your schedule, while you keep working on CPT.

Week 1

On campus

Open the term in person with faculty and your cohort in Los Angeles or San Diego.

Weeks 2–7

Online & working

Complete coursework online around your job. This is where the DBA earns its keep: the business problems you research are the ones on your desk, and your employer gets the answers as you produce them.

Week 8

On campus

Close the term in person with presentations, assessments, and cohort sessions.

DBA or PhD?

Both are doctorates. They are not the same job.

This confuses a lot of people from outside the U.S., where "doctorate" often just means PhD. Both earn you the title of Doctor - they simply point at different futures.

This program

DBA - the applied doctorate

  • Applied research on real business problems, often inside your own company
  • Built around your job: hybrid, evenings and weekends around campus sessions
  • Aimed at executive leadership, consulting, and entrepreneurship
  • Roughly three years, working full-time the entire way
  • Day 1 CPT with no annual cap, then a fresh period of OPT
The alternative

PhD - the academic doctorate

  • Theoretical research produced for scholarly journals
  • Years on campus, typically full-time study
  • Aimed at a professorship and an academic research career
  • Longer timeline, with far less room for a parallel career
  • Also a doctorate - just a different destination
Short version: if you want to keep working and advance your career, the DBA is the doctorate built for you. If your goal is a research and teaching career, ask us about the PhD in Organizational Leadership instead - an advisor will tell you honestly which one fits.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is a DBA?
DBA stands for Doctor of Business Administration. It is a doctorate, the highest degree level in business and a full step above every master's degree including the MBA. Graduates hold the title of Doctor. Unlike a traditional academic doctorate, the DBA is designed for working professionals: you research and solve real business problems while continuing your career, on an executive schedule with campus sessions every 8 weeks.
Is a DBA the same thing as a PhD?
No, and this confuses a lot of people from outside the U.S., where "doctorate" often just means PhD. Both are doctoral degrees and both earn you the title of Doctor, but they serve different goals. A PhD trains full-time academics: years on campus producing theoretical research for scholarly journals, usually aiming at a professorship. A DBA applies that same doctoral rigor to the real world: applied research on actual business problems, built around your job, aimed at executive leadership and consulting.
Do I need an MBA to apply?
No. Any accredited master's degree qualifies you. The MBA-to-DBA route is simply the most common among our students. Whatever master's you hold, the doctorate is the next degree level up, and that is what makes you eligible for a new period of OPT after you graduate.
Can I really work through the entire program?
Yes, and this is where this DBA stands apart. Many universities limit how much CPT you can use per year or per degree level, forcing students to pause their jobs mid-program. Here, your CPT runs from day one straight through graduation - roughly three years of uninterrupted, full-time work authorization in your field. And because the doctorate is a higher degree level than your master's, you qualify for a fresh period of OPT after you finish.
What does the program cost?
It depends on you, in a good way. Eligible students receive up to a $15,000 grant toward tuition, transfer credits from your master's reduce it further, and the remainder is spread across an interest-free, pay-as-you-go plan designed to be covered while you work. An advisor will give you your exact numbers in your free consultation, based on your transcript and grant eligibility.
When does the DBA start, and what is the format?
Most students finish in approximately three years, depending on pace, transfer credits, and dissertation progress. The program runs on 8-week terms with a hybrid executive format: on campus twice every 8 weeks in Los Angeles or San Diego, with the rest built around your work schedule. Intakes open in January, March, May, July, August, and October. If you are finishing a master's or OPT, register 3–6 months ahead so your start date lines up with no gap in status. To plan ahead, you can find a copy of the academic calendar here.
I'm on OPT right now. How does the transition work?
This is the most common path. Register for the DBA 3–6 months before your OPT ends; we handle the SEVIS transfer or change of status so your student status continues seamlessly. Your Day 1 CPT activates on the first day of class, meaning you can keep the same job without interruption.
What if I don't have a job, or my employer isn't set up for CPT?
Either way, you are covered. If you already have an employer, we make the arrangement compliant so you keep working from day one. If you don't, CareerConnect, our CPT/OPT work placement assistance, matches you with a paid position aligned with your degree, with resume support and employer introductions before your program starts.
What scholarships and financial aid are available?
Eligible applicants may receive a $15,000 tuition grant and transfer up to 12 master's-level credits to further reduce tuition. The remaining balance can be paid through an interest-free, pay-as-you-go payment plan designed to be manageable while you work.
