Overview

What is an Extension of Stay?

An Extension of Stay (EOS) lets eligible nonimmigrants remain in the United States beyond the date listed on their Form I-94, without changing their visa category. Filing on time is critical — falling out of status can affect future applications.

Eligibility

Who Can Apply?

You may qualify for an Extension of Stay if all of the following apply to your situation.

  • You were lawfully admitted to the U.S. in a nonimmigrant status.
  • Your nonimmigrant status remains valid at the time of filing.
  • You have not committed any acts that make you ineligible.
  • You have not violated the conditions of your admission.
  • Your passport is valid and will remain valid for the duration of your stay.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How early should I file my Extension of Stay?

USCIS recommends filing at least 45 days before your I-94 expires. Earlier is better — we file as soon as your packet is ready.

Can I stay in the U.S. while my I-539 is pending?

Yes. If you file before your I-94 expires, you may remain in the U.S. for up to 240 days while your case is being processed (specific rules vary by category).

What happens if my extension is denied?

If denied, you must leave the U.S. by the date in the denial notice. We'll review the decision and discuss appeal or refile options when available.

How long does USCIS take to process Form I-539?

Processing times vary by service center and visa category — typically 2 to 8 months. Premium processing is available for certain categories.

Do I need a lawyer to file?

No, but professional guidance reduces errors and delays. Our team has filed thousands of successful nonimmigrant applications.
