The I-539 form is the USCIS application you file to switch to F-1 student status without leaving the United States. You stay in the country while USCIS decides it, and you can file online or by mail. Most problems with an I-539 application come from timing and small filing mistakes, not from the rules themselves.

This guide walks through how to file the I-539 form, what affects I-539 processing time and how to avoid the mistakes that lead to delays and denials. For background on the full change of status process, see our F-1 change of status guide.

Quick answer

To switch to F-1 status inside the U.S., you get an initial I-20 from an SEVP-certified school, pay the SEVIS fee and file Form I-539 with USCIS before your current status expires. The most common problems are filing too late, starting classes before approval and processing times that push you past your I-20 start date. Weak financial evidence is a frequent cause of requests for evidence. You can avoid most of these by filing early, choosing a school with frequent start dates and getting legal advice from a licensed immigration attorney before you file.

What the I-539 form is and who files it

USCIS Form I-539, Application to Extend/Change Nonimmigrant Status, is used by people already in the U.S. who want more time in their current status or a switch to a different one. For students, it is how you change from a status like B-2, H-4 or J-1 to F-1 without going home for a visa interview.

You can only use it if you are in the U.S. in a valid status. You also have to file it before the date on your I-94 record. USCIS excuses a late filing only in very limited cases, such as extraordinary circumstances beyond your control that caused a reasonable delay.

Which edition of the I-539 form to use right now

As of October 2026, USCIS accepts the 08/28/24 edition of Form I-539. A revised 09/15/26 edition was tied to the new fixed admission period rule for F-1 students, and USCIS is not accepting it under a September 14, 2026 court order. Read more in our post on the duration of status rule changes.

The edition date is printed at the bottom of every page. If you mail a paper form, every page has to come from the same edition, or USCIS may reject it.

USCIS also changed the I-539 filing locations in October 2026. Packages sent to a prior location are still accepted if postmarked on or before November 9, 2026. Check the mailing address on the USCIS I-539 page the day you file, and never copy it from an old checklist. Filing online avoids the address question entirely.

How to file the I-539 form, step by step

Each step below includes the mistake that most often trips students up at that point.

1. Confirm you are eligible to file

You must have been lawfully admitted, your current status must still be valid and you must not have violated its terms, for example by working without authorization.

Watch out: people admitted under the Visa Waiver Program (ESTA) cannot change status in the U.S. Some J-1 holders subject to the two-year home residency requirement cannot change to F-1 either.

2. Get admitted and receive an initial I-20

Your school's Designated School Official (DSO) issues an initial I-20 with "change of status" as the reason. The school must be SEVP-certified.

Watch out: your financial documents must cover the amount on the I-20. Weak or outdated bank statements are one of the most common reasons USCIS asks for more evidence.

3. Pay the I-901 SEVIS fee

Keep the payment receipt. It goes in your I-539 application.

Watch out: the SEVIS fee is tied to your I-20's SEVIS ID. If the school issues a new I-20 with a new ID, check with the DSO before paying again.

4. File the I-539 form before your I-94 date

File online through a USCIS account or mail a paper application. Include your I-20, SEVIS fee receipt, I-94, passport, financial evidence and a copy of your current visa. Follow the USCIS I-539 instructions for your category, and confirm the current fee and edition on the USCIS I-539 page.

Watch out: your filing date is the day USCIS receives a complete application, not the day you mail it. If your status expires before then, USCIS generally cannot approve the change. This is the one mistake that is very hard to fix.

5. Add premium processing if your start date is close

You can request premium processing on Form I-907, with your I-539 or after filing. See the I-539 processing time section below for the timeframe and fee.

Watch out: premium processing speeds up the decision, not the outcome. A weak application is still weak.

6. Stay in the U.S. and stay in status while you wait

Do not leave the country while your I-539 is pending. Travel abroad generally abandons the application.

Watch out: if you are in B-1/B-2 status, do not enroll or start classes until USCIS approves the change. Doing so is a status violation.

7. Respond quickly to any request for evidence

Answer by the deadline on the notice and send exactly what was asked for, organized and labeled.

8. Start your program once approved

Your F-1 status begins on the date in your approval notice. Contact your DSO right away so they can update your SEVIS record.

Watch out: if approval comes after your program start date, your DSO will defer you to a later term. Study in the States confirms that F-1 applicants whose status was valid at filing do not need extra filings to bridge the gap to the new start date.

I-539 processing time

I-539 processing time for a change to F-1 status can run several months, and it changes often. Any number you see on a forum or a blog, including this one, may be out of date by the time you file.

How to check the current I-539 processing time

Use the processing times tool on the USCIS website. Select Form I-539 and the office listed on your receipt notice. The receipt notice arrives after USCIS accepts your application, and its receipt number also lets you track the case online.

If you have not filed yet, look up the current time before you pick a program start date. That one check prevents most missed start dates.

What slows an I-539 down

A request for evidence adds weeks, because the case waits until USCIS gets your response. Incomplete financial documents are the most common trigger. Filing close to your program start date does not slow USCIS down, but it leaves you no room if anything else does.

Premium processing for the I-539

Change of status requests to F-1 are eligible for premium processing through Form I-907. The fee rose to $2,075 on March 1, 2026. USCIS commits to taking action within 30 business days once all prerequisites are met, which can include biometrics.

Taking action means an approval, a denial or a request for evidence. If USCIS issues a request for evidence, a new 30-business-day period starts once it receives your response.

I-539 fee

The I-539 fee depends on how you file and can change with the USCIS fee schedule, so check the current amount on the USCIS I-539 page before you pay. The fee is separate from the I-901 SEVIS fee and from the optional premium processing fee. If you file online, USCIS collects the fee when you submit, which removes one common reason paper filings are rejected.

Common I-539 problems and how to avoid them

Filing too late

USCIS can only change your status if it is still valid when your application arrives. If your I-94 date has passed or you have worked without authorization, you are out of status, and USCIS generally cannot approve the change. Check your I-94 date on the CBP website the day you decide to switch, and file with time to spare.

Using the wrong form edition or address

A paper I-539 on an edition USCIS is not accepting, or mailed to the wrong location, can be rejected and sent back. If that happens close to your I-94 date, you may not have time to refile. Check both on the USCIS I-539 page the day you file, or file online.

Missing your program start date

If your I-539 is still pending when your I-20 start date arrives, your DSO will defer you to a later term. That is not a denial, but it can push your studies back a semester at schools with only two or three start dates a year. A school with frequent start dates shortens that wait.

Starting classes or work too early

Enrolling while you are still in B-1/B-2 status is a status violation, even if your application is pending. The same goes for starting CPT or any other F-1 activity before approval. Wait for the approval notice, then confirm your start date with the DSO.

B-1/B-2 visitors and the question of intent

If you entered as a visitor, USCIS may ask whether you planned to study before you arrived. The State Department uses a 90-day guideline for conduct inconsistent with your visa, and many school advisors recommend waiting at least 90 days after entry before filing. USCIS is not bound by that guideline, but a filing soon after arrival invites closer questions. Talk to an immigration attorney about your timeline before you file.

Requests for evidence

Requests for evidence on an I-539 for F-1 status often ask for clearer proof of funds or proof that you maintained your current status. Send complete, current bank statements and sponsor letters the first time, and include a short statement explaining why the program fits your background.

Traveling while the I-539 is pending

Leaving the U.S. while your I-539 is pending generally abandons it. If you must travel, the alternative is to withdraw the application, apply for an F-1 visa at a U.S. consulate abroad and re-enter as a student. Decide which route you are taking before you book anything.

What to do if your I-539 is denied

USCIS sends a denial notice that explains why the application was refused. Read it closely before you decide anything, because the reason shapes your options.

You generally cannot appeal an I-539 denial. You can ask USCIS to look at the case again by filing Form I-290B, usually within 30 days of the decision. A motion to reopen is for new facts or evidence. A motion to reconsider argues that USCIS applied the rules incorrectly. Filing a motion does not keep you in status or let you remain in the U.S. while it is decided.

If your previous status has already expired, unlawful presence can start the day after the denial. Time out of status can follow you into future visa applications, so leaving on time often matters more than contesting the decision.

Many students leave and apply for an F-1 visa at a U.S. consulate instead. Your DSO can usually issue an updated I-20 with a later start date, so a denial does not have to end your plans to study. An immigration attorney can tell you which route fits your history.

Who can legally help with your I-539

You are allowed to file the I-539 form on your own, and many students do. Most problems, though, come from decisions made before the form is filed: when to file, which start date to target and whether to change status in the U.S. or travel for a visa instead. That is where support pays off.

According to USCIS, only a licensed attorney or an accredited representative of a Department of Justice recognized organization can give you legal advice or represent you on an immigration case. A notario, notary public or immigration consultant cannot, no matter what they advertise.

In practice, reliable support usually comes from a few places working together:

  • A licensed immigration attorney reviews your history, advises on timing and risk, and prepares or reviews the filing.
  • Your school's DSO issues the I-20, sets and defers your program start date, and explains the F-1 rules once you are approved.
  • An enrollment partner such as EduConnect helps you pick the right program and start date, gets your admission and I-20 moving quickly, and keeps the school side of your timeline on track.

Signs an agency is not reliable

  • It guarantees approval or promises to speed up USCIS. Only USCIS controls processing, apart from paid premium processing.
  • The person giving you legal advice is not a licensed attorney or accredited representative, or will not say which they are.
  • It asks for your original documents or tells you to sign a blank or incomplete form.
  • It tells you to start classes or work before your approval arrives.

Can I start classes while my I-539 is pending?
