First Things First

What exactly is a DBA?

DBA stands for Doctor of Business Administration. It is a doctorate, the highest degree level in business, one full level above every master’s degree, including the MBA. If you are from outside the U.S., you may only know one kind of doctorate, the PhD. They are not the same, and the difference matters.

DBA

Doctor of Business Administration

The doctorate built for working professionals who want the highest degree in business without leaving their career.

  • Applied research that solves real business problems
  • Keep your job: on campus every 8 weeks, the rest fits around work
  • About 3 years while working full-time the entire way
  • Leads to executive leadership, consulting, and boardrooms
PhD

Doctor of Philosophy

The doctorate built for full-time academics who want to research and teach at a university.

  • Theoretical research written for scholarly journals
  • Full-time on campus, with little room for a career outside
  • Typically 4-6+ years to complete
  • Leads to professorships and academic tenure

Both earn you the title of Doctor, and both sit a full degree level above your master’s. That higher level is what unlocks your new period of work authorization. And you do not need an MBA: any accredited master’s degree qualifies you for the next level up.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a DBA, in plain English?

DBA stands for Doctor of Business Administration. It is a doctorate, the highest degree level in business, a full step above every master’s degree including the MBA. Graduates hold the title of Doctor. Unlike a traditional academic doctorate, the DBA is designed for working professionals: you research and solve real business problems while continuing your career, on an executive schedule with campus sessions every 8 weeks in Los Angeles.

Is a DBA the same thing as a PhD?

No, and this confuses a lot of people from outside the U.S., where “doctorate” often just means PhD. Both are doctoral degrees and both earn you the title of Doctor, but they serve different goals. A PhD trains full-time academics: years on campus producing theoretical research for scholarly journals, usually aiming at a professorship. A DBA applies that same doctoral rigor to the real world: applied research on actual business problems, built around your job, aimed at executive leadership and consulting. If you want to keep working and advance your career, the DBA is the doctorate built for you.

Do I need an MBA to apply?

No. Any accredited master’s degree qualifies you. The MBA-to-DBA route is simply the most common among our students. Whatever master’s you hold, the doctorate is the next degree level up, and that is what makes you eligible for a new period of OPT after you graduate.

Can I really work through the entire program?

Yes, and this is where this DBA stands apart. Many universities limit how much CPT you can use per year or per degree level, forcing students to pause their jobs mid-program. Here, your CPT runs from day one straight through graduation, roughly 3 years of uninterrupted, full-time work authorization in your field. And because the doctorate is a higher degree level than your master’s, you qualify for a fresh period of OPT after you finish.

What does the program cost?

It depends on you, in a good way. Eligible students receive up to a $15,000 Grant toward tuition, transfer credits from your master’s reduce it further, and the remainder is spread across an interest-free, pay-as-you-go plan designed to be covered while you work. An advisor will give you your exact numbers in your free consultation, based on your transcript and Grant eligibility.

When does the DBA start, and what is the format?

Most students finish in approximately 3 years, depending on pace, transfer credits, and dissertation progress. The program runs on 8-week terms with a hybrid executive format: on campus in Los Angeles every 8 weeks, with the rest built around your work schedule. Intakes open in January, March, May, July, August, and October. If you are finishing a master’s or OPT, register 3-6 months ahead so your start date lines up with no gap in status. You can review the academic calendar to plan ahead.

I’m on OPT right now. How does the transition work?

This is the most common path. Register for the DBA 3-6 months before your OPT ends; we handle the SEVIS transfer or change of status so your student status continues seamlessly. Your Day 1 CPT activates on the first day of class, meaning you can keep the same job without interruption.

What if I don’t have a job, or my employer isn’t set up for CPT?

Either way, you are covered. If you already have an employer, we make the arrangement compliant so you keep working from day one. If you don’t, CareerConnect, our CPT/OPT work placement assistance, matches you with a paid position aligned with your degree, with resume support and employer introductions before your program starts.
