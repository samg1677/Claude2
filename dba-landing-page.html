First Things First

What exactly is a DBA?

DBA stands for Doctor of Business Administration. It is a doctorate — the highest level of degree you can earn in business, one full level above every master's degree, including the MBA. When you finish, you hold the title of Doctor.

If you're not from the U.S., you may have only heard of one kind of doctorate: the PhD. A DBA is not a PhD, and that's the point. A PhD in business is built for people who want to become full-time academics — researchers and professors who spend years on campus producing theory. The DBA takes the same doctoral rigor and points it at the real world: you research and solve actual business problems, often the ones inside the company you already work for.

That's why the DBA is built around a career, not instead of one. The executive hybrid format brings you to the Los Angeles campus every 8 weeks; the rest fits around your job. Most students finish in about 3 years while working full-time the entire way.

And you don't need an MBA to apply — any accredited master's degree qualifies you for the next level up.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a DBA, in plain English?

DBA stands for Doctor of Business Administration. It's a doctorate — the highest degree level in business, a full step above every master's degree including the MBA. Graduates hold the title of Doctor. Unlike a traditional academic doctorate, the DBA is designed for working professionals: you research and solve real business problems while continuing your career, on an executive schedule with campus sessions every 8 weeks in Los Angeles.

Is a DBA the same thing as a PhD?

No — and this confuses a lot of people from outside the U.S., where "doctorate" often just means PhD. Both are doctoral degrees and both earn you the title of Doctor, but they serve different goals. A PhD trains full-time academics: years on campus producing theoretical research for scholarly journals, usually aiming at a professorship. A DBA applies that same doctoral rigor to the real world: applied research on actual business problems, built around your job, aimed at executive leadership and consulting. If you want to keep working and advance your career, the DBA is the doctorate built for you.

Do I need an MBA to apply?

No. Any accredited master's degree qualifies you — the MBA-to-DBA route is simply the most common among our students. Whatever master's you hold, the doctorate is the next degree level up, and that's what makes you eligible for a new period of OPT when you graduate.

Can I really work through the entire program?

Yes — and this is where this DBA stands apart. Many universities limit how much CPT you can use per year or per degree level, forcing students to pause their jobs mid-program. Here, your CPT runs from day one straight through graduation — roughly 3 years of uninterrupted, full-time work authorization in your field. And because the doctorate is a higher degree level than your master's, you qualify for a fresh period of OPT after you finish.

What does the program cost?

It depends on you — in a good way. Eligible students receive up to a $15,000 Grant toward tuition, transfer credits from your master's reduce it further, and the remainder is spread across an interest-free, pay-as-you-go plan designed to be covered while you work. An advisor will give you your exact numbers in your free consultation, based on your transcript and Grant eligibility.

How long does the DBA take, and what's the format?

Most students finish in approximately 3 years, depending on pace, transfer credits, and dissertation progress. The program runs on 8-week terms with a hybrid executive format: on campus in Los Angeles every 8 weeks, with the rest built around your work schedule. Intakes open in January, March, May, July, August, and October — if you're finishing a master's or OPT, register 3–6 months ahead so your start date lines up with no gap in status.

I'm on OPT right now. How does the transition work?

This is the most common path. Register for the DBA 3–6 months before your OPT ends; we handle the SEVIS transfer or change of status so your student status continues seamlessly. Your Day 1 CPT activates on the first day of class — meaning you can keep the same job without interruption.

What if I don't have a job, or my employer isn't set up for CPT?

Either way, you're covered. If you already have an employer, we make the arrangement compliant so you keep working from day one. If you don't, CareerConnect — our CPT/OPT work placement assistance — matches you with a paid position aligned with your degree, with resume support and employer introductions before your program starts.
