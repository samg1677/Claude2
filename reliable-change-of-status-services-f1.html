Changing to F-1 student status inside the U.S. is a single USCIS application, but a lot rides on it. If it goes wrong, you can lose months of study time or have to leave the country. That is why so many students look for an agency to help, and why it matters who you choose.

This guide explains what reliable change of status services actually look like and how to spot an agency you should avoid.

Which agencies provide reliable change of status services to F-1 student visa?

Quick answer

Reliable change of status support for an F-1 student visa usually comes from two sources working together. For legal advice and the Form I-539 filing, use a licensed immigration attorney or an accredited representative of a Department of Justice recognized organization. USCIS says only they can advise you on your case. For the school side, work with your school's international office or its official enrollment partner. At Alliant University, that partner is EduConnect USA, which helps students plan their program, start date and I-20 timing around USCIS processing.

Avoid any agency that guarantees approval or a processing time. Only USCIS decides your case, and only USCIS controls how fast it moves.

What reliable change of status services cover

A reliable provider, or team of providers, should be comfortable with every part of an F-1 change of status. Use this as a checklist when you compare options.

Form I-539 and supporting evidence

The change of status request is filed on Form I-539 with USCIS, along with your I-20, SEVIS fee receipt, I-94, passport and financial evidence. Someone on your side should review the whole package before it goes in.

Your current status

A J-1, a B-1/B-2 visitor and a laid-off H-1B worker each face different risks. J-1 holders need to check the two-year home residency requirement. Visitors face questions about whether they meant to study when they entered. Your provider should have handled your starting status before.

Timing and maintenance of status

Most change of status problems are timing problems. Your current status has to be valid when USCIS receives the application, and you have to keep it valid while you wait. A good provider plans backward from your I-94 date and your program start date.

I-20 and SEVIS requirements

Your I-20 must be issued for change of status, and your financial documents must match the amount on it. If your start date is deferred because USCIS is still processing, the school issues an updated I-20.

Requests for evidence and complicated histories

A request for evidence (RFE) is common and is not a denial. Past overstays, prior visa denials or several changes of status in a row need an attorney's review before anything is filed.

The two kinds of help you need

 

Most students search for one agency that does everything. In practice, reliable change of status support splits into two roles, and knowing the difference protects you.

Legal help: a licensed immigration attorney

According to USCIS, only a licensed attorney or an accredited representative of a DOJ-recognized organization can give you legal advice or represent you on an immigration case. A notario, notary public or immigration consultant cannot, whatever their advertising says.

An attorney is the right person to review your immigration history and decide whether a change of status or a trip abroad for a visa is the safer route. They can also prepare or review your I-539. You can check an attorney's license through your state bar's website.

School-side help: your school or its enrollment partner

The other half of your application comes from the school. Your school's Designated School Official (DSO) issues the I-20 and can defer your program start date if USCIS runs long. An official enrollment partner helps you choose a program and a start date that fit your USCIS timeline, then keeps your admission and I-20 moving.

This is the part many students overlook. A well-prepared I-539 can still run into trouble if the program start date is unrealistic or the I-20 does not match your finances.

Red flags that a change of status agency is not reliable

  • It guarantees approval or quotes a success rate as if it were a promise. USCIS decides every case, and no agency controls the outcome.
  • It promises to speed up USCIS. The only way to get a faster decision is paid premium processing through Form I-907, which you can request yourself.
  • The person giving you legal advice is not a licensed attorney or accredited representative, or will not say which they are.
  • It asks for your original documents, or asks you to sign a blank or incomplete form.
  • It tells you to start classes or a job before your change of status is approved.
  • It never asks about your I-94 date, your past statuses or any prior visa denials.
  • It cannot tell you which school will issue your I-20 or when your program starts.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

Ask these on your first call. A reliable provider will answer them plainly.

  1. Are you a licensed attorney or DOJ-accredited representative? If not, who handles the legal side of my case?
  2. Have you worked with students changing from my current status before?
  3. When does my current status end, and what start date do you recommend given current USCIS processing times?
  4. What happens if USCIS has not decided by my program start date?
  5. Who handles a request for evidence if I get one, and is that included in the fee?
  6. Which school will issue my I-20, and are you connected to that school officially?
  7. What exactly does your fee cover, and what costs extra, including USCIS fees and premium processing?

Can an immigration consultant file my change of status?

A consultant or notario cannot give you legal advice or represent you before USCIS. Only a licensed attorney or DOJ-accredited representative can do that. You are allowed to file Form I-539 yourself.

How do I know if a change of status agency is legitimate?

Ask whether the person advising you is a licensed attorney or accredited representative, and check the attorney's license with the state bar. A legitimate agency will be clear about what its fee covers and will not guarantee approval.
