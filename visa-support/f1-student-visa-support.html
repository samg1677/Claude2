H-1B Alternatives

Not selected in the H-1B lottery? F-1 keeps your U.S. career moving

The H-1B lottery is unpredictable, and every year many qualified professionals are left without a plan. Enrolling in an F-1 graduate program is one of the most practical H-1B alternatives: you earn an advanced degree, keep working in your field through Day 1 CPT, and may qualify for OPT and the STEM OPT extension after graduation — giving you more chances at the lottery in future years.

  • Current H-1B, H-4 or OPT holders can plan a timely move to F-1
  • STEM-designated master's degrees in Data Analytics, Healthcare Analytics and Information Systems
  • Hybrid schedules so you can keep working while you study

Student Visa Questions

F1 Student Visa FAQ

What is an F-1 student visa?

The F-1 visa is the U.S. academic student visa. It allows international students to enroll full-time in a degree program at a SEVP-certified U.S. college or university and, with authorization, gain work experience through CPT and OPT.

Can I change my status to F-1 without leaving the United States?

In many cases, yes. Eligible J-1, B-1/B-2 and other nonimmigrant visa holders can apply to USCIS for a change of status to F-1 using Form I-539 while they are still in valid status. Eligibility depends on your current visa, so your case should be reviewed before you apply.

Can I change from a J-1 visa to an F-1 visa?

Many J-1 holders can change to F-1 status. However, if you are subject to the two-year home residency requirement (212(e)), you generally cannot change status inside the U.S. unless you first obtain a waiver. See our J-1 to F-1 change of status guide.

Can I change from a B-1/B-2 visitor visa to an F-1 visa?

Yes, visitors can request a change of status to F-1 from inside the U.S., but you may not begin full-time study until USCIS approves the change. Learn more on our B-1/B-2 to F-1 page.

What is a SEVIS transfer?

A SEVIS transfer moves your F-1 student record from your current school to a new SEVP-certified school, so you can continue studying without leaving the U.S. or applying for a new visa. Read our SEVIS transfer guide.

Is the F-1 visa a good alternative to the H-1B?

For many professionals who were not selected in the H-1B lottery, an F-1 graduate degree offers a lawful way to stay in the U.S., keep building experience through CPT, and become eligible for OPT and, in STEM-designated programs, the 24-month STEM OPT extension.

Can I work in the U.S. on an F-1 visa?

Yes, with authorization. Through our partner Alliant University, eligible students can access Day 1 CPT, which lets you work in your field from your first term. After graduation, you may qualify for 12 months of OPT plus a 24-month STEM OPT extension in STEM-designated programs.

What documents do I need for an F-1 visa?

Typical documents include a valid passport, a Form I-20, proof of SEVIS I-901 fee payment, proof of financial support, academic transcripts and, for change of status, Form I-539 and your current I-94 record. Applicants abroad also complete the DS-160 and attend a consular interview.
