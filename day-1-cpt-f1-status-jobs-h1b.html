September 30, 2026 · 9 min read · Day 1 CPT

Day 1 CPT programs let F-1 graduate students work full time from their first term, and for many international students that is the whole point of enrolling. But CPT is tied to your F-1 status, and how you use it shapes your options later, from OPT to an H-1B petition.

This guide explains what Day 1 CPT does to your status and your job prospects, and what to plan for before you pick a program.

Quick answer

Day 1 CPT does not hurt your F-1 status when it is used correctly. It keeps you in status and working while you study, as long as CPT is a required part of your program and your DSO authorizes each job and date range on your I-20.

The tradeoffs are real, though. Twelve months or more of full-time CPT ends your OPT eligibility for that degree level, and USCIS often asks Day 1 CPT students to prove they maintained status when they later file for an H-1B. Clean records and a well-documented program make that step much easier.

How Day 1 CPT affects your F-1 visa status

CPT is not a separate visa or status. It is permission to work that your school's Designated School Official (DSO) adds to your I-20 while you remain an F-1 student. That means your right to work depends entirely on staying in good F-1 standing.

What keeps you in status on Day 1 CPT

  • Stay enrolled full time and attend every required session, including in-person classes.
  • Work only for the employer and only within the dates your DSO authorized. Each new job or date range needs a new CPT authorization before you start.
  • Keep your job directly related to your major. A marketing role fits an MBA in marketing, and a data engineering role fits a data analytics degree.
  • Keep your GPA and academic progress in line with your school's requirements.

What changed in August 2026

The CPT regulations did not change in 2026, but how SEVP reads them did. In two broadcast messages sent on August 12 and August 24, 2026, SEVP told school officials to authorize CPT only when practical training is an integral, required part of the curriculum. The August 24 message says that when practical training is integral to a program, every student in that program must take part, U.S. and international alike. An optional elective does not meet that standard.

For you, this means the school's program design matters more than it used to. A school that treats CPT as an add-on is a risk to your status, even if your own paperwork is perfect.

What about duration of status?

A DHS rule that would have replaced duration of status with fixed admission periods was set to take effect on September 15, 2026. A federal court postponed it on September 14, so F-1 students are still admitted for duration of status while the litigation continues. Check for updates before you commit to a long program.

How Day 1 CPT programs support full-time jobs

Day 1 CPT is one of the few ways an F-1 student can work full time during a degree program. OPT usually requires a USCIS application and a wait, while CPT is authorized by your DSO, often within days of your program start. That speed is why Day 1 CPT is popular with students whose OPT is ending and with workers changing status after a layoff.

For employers, a CPT student is usually simple to hire. There is no petition to file, and the employer mainly needs to confirm the job details for your CPT authorization. The catch is that the role has to line up with your degree, so the program you choose limits the jobs you can take.

Full-time CPT and OPT: the tradeoff

 

If you use 12 months or more of full-time CPT during a degree program, you lose OPT eligibility for that degree level. Part-time CPT, meaning 20 hours a week or less, does not count toward that limit.

If full-time work throughout the program matters most, you can use full-time CPT for the whole program and accept that you will not get OPT from that degree.

If you want OPT later, and STEM OPT on top of it, keep full-time CPT under 12 months in total or mix in part-time CPT.

OPT eligibility resets at a higher degree level. A student who uses full-time CPT during a master's can still qualify for OPT after a doctorate. Work this out with your DSO before you enroll, not halfway through.

Day 1 CPT and your H-1B sponsorship prospects

Day 1 CPT does not disqualify you from an H-1B. Many students use it for exactly that reason: it keeps them working for an employer who can register them in the next lottery. What it does change is how closely USCIS looks at your record when that petition is filed.

Why USCIS looks closer at Day 1 CPT students

When your employer files an H-1B petition that changes your status inside the U.S., USCIS has to confirm you maintained F-1 status up to that point. For Day 1 CPT students, requests for evidence often focus on maintenance of status. USCIS may ask for your CPT I-20s, proof of enrollment and attendance, and evidence that your CPT was integral to your program and related to your job.

That is why your records matter as much as your degree. Keep these from day one:

  • Every I-20 showing a CPT authorization, with employer names and dates
  • Transcripts, enrollment verifications and attendance records for in-person sessions
  • Pay stubs that match your authorized CPT dates, with no work during gaps between authorizations
  • Your offer letter and a job description that ties your duties to your coursework

One more flag to avoid: moving through several Day 1 CPT programs at the same degree level without a clear academic reason can look like a program used only for work authorization. A logical progression, such as a master's followed by a doctorate, is easier to explain.

What changed in the H-1B lottery for 2026

Starting with the FY 2027 cap season, USCIS no longer runs a purely random lottery. Under a final rule effective February 27, 2026, registrations get more entries at higher wage levels, so a job offered at wage level III or IV has a much better chance of selection than an entry-level offer. Selection is still possible at every level.

This makes the job you hold on CPT more important. A role with real responsibility and a salary above entry level improves your odds in a way that a placeholder job does not.

The $100,000 H-1B fee from the September 2025 presidential proclamation targets certain petitions for workers outside the U.S. USCIS guidance says it does not apply when an F-1 student inside the U.S. is approved for a change of status to H-1B. The fee is also being challenged in court, so check with your employer's attorney before registration season.

Why choosing a Day 1 CPT program feels confusing

 

Most of the confusion comes from two things. The rules are strict but the marketing is loose, and the right choice depends on plans you may not have finished making yet.

Schools and agents describe the same rules in very different ways. Some present CPT as an easy work permit and skip the OPT tradeoff entirely. On-site requirements, CPT renewal schedules and start dates vary from school to school, and much of what you read online was written before SEVP's August 2026 guidance.

The decision also reaches years ahead. The program you pick affects which jobs qualify for CPT and whether you keep OPT. It also shapes how your record reads when an employer files an H-1B for you.

How to choose a Day 1 CPT program

1. Start with your job.

Pick the degree your current or target role clearly relates to, since CPT has to match your major.

2. Decide on OPT before you enroll.

If you want OPT or STEM OPT later, plan your full-time CPT to stay under 12 months.

3. Ask the DSO how CPT is built into the curriculum.

You want to hear that it is required for every student in the program and documented as such.

4. Check accreditation.

The school should be accredited by an agency the U.S. Department of Education recognizes.

5. Look at the schedule you will live with.

Campus location, in-person requirements and start dates matter more over two years than they do on an application.

6. Ask what career support you get.

If you do not have a qualifying job yet, placement help is worth more than a lower on-site requirement.
