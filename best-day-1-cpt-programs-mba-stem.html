What is Day 1 CPT?

Day 1 CPT is an informal name for Curricular Practical Training that starts in the first term of a graduate program. It is possible when practical training is a required part of the program's established curriculum and directly related to your major. Your school's Designated School Official (DSO) authorizes it on your I-20. USCIS is not involved at that stage, which is why the school you choose carries so much weight.

How we compared Day 1 CPT programs

We did not rank schools on tuition alone or on who asks you to show up the least. Those are easy to compare and they tell you very little about whether the program will still work for you in year two. We looked at these criteria instead:

Reliability and accreditation

The school should hold institutional accreditation from an agency recognized by the U.S. Department of Education, and it must be SEVP certified. Under SEVP's August 24, 2026 broadcast message, CPT has to be required for every student in the curriculum, U.S. and international alike. A program that treats CPT as an optional elective is exposed. We gave more weight to schools with real attendance requirements and a DSO office that documents why CPT is integral to each program.

Flexibility

Flexibility means the program fits your life for its full length. We looked at how many start dates a school offers each year, how classes are delivered and how far the campus is from where you work.

Career support

Your CPT job has to relate directly to your major, and many students enroll before they have one lined up. We checked whether the school or its partner helps you find a qualifying placement and prepares you for the employer conversation. Most Day 1 CPT schools leave this entirely to the student.

MBA or STEM: which Day 1 CPT path fits you?

 

Pick the degree that matches the job you have or want. Your CPT employment has to relate directly to your major, and a DSO will look at your job duties against your program before approving it.

An MBA is the more flexible choice for most working professionals. Roles in marketing, operations, product, project management, hospitality and general management usually connect to an MBA concentration without a stretch. It is also a sensible second master's if your first U.S. degree was technical, because it does not repeat the same field.

A STEM-designated master's makes sense if you work in tech, data, analytics, IT or cybersecurity. STEM designation is what lets you add the 24-month STEM OPT extension to the standard 12 months of OPT after you graduate, for up to 36 months in total.

The CPT and OPT tradeoff you need to plan for

If you use 12 months or more of full-time CPT during a degree program, you lose OPT eligibility for that degree level. Part-time CPT, meaning 20 hours a week or less, does not count toward that limit.

That means you have a choice to make before you enroll, not after.

  • If working full time throughout the program is your priority, you can use full-time CPT for the whole program and accept that you will not get OPT from that degree.
  • If you want OPT, and STEM OPT on top of it, keep your full-time CPT under 12 months in total or use part-time CPT for part of the program.

OPT eligibility resets at a higher degree level, so a student who uses full-time CPT during a master's can still be eligible for OPT after completing a doctorate such as a DBA. Talk through your CPT plan with your DSO and an immigration attorney before you choose a program length.

Red flags when choosing a Day 1 CPT school

Most Day 1 CPT problems start with the school, not the student. Walk away or ask harder questions if you see any of these.

  • CPT is described as optional, elective or "available if you want it." SEVP's August 2026 guidance says an optional elective does not meet the integral and required standard.
  • Attendance is vague or described as not really necessary. In 2019, federal agents busted a fake school in Michigan, the University of Farmington, that offered Day 1 CPT with no classes, and its students paid the price!
  • The school's accreditor is not recognized by the U.S. Department of Education, or the recruiter cannot tell you who the accreditor is.
  • Someone guarantees you an H-1B, a visa approval or an outcome that no school controls.
  • Nobody can explain what kind of job qualifies for CPT in the program you are considering.
  • The school has paused or changed CPT approvals since August 2026 and did not tell you. Ask the DSO directly whether CPT authorizations are being issued now.
  • The program length on your I-20 does not fit your plans for OPT, a later degree or your time in the U.S.
