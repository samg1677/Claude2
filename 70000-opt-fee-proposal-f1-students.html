The $70,000 OPT Fee: What the DHS Proposal Means for F-1 Students

October 13, 2026 · 8 min read · F-1 Rule Changes

Last updated: October 13, 2026

Quick answer

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has proposed a $70,000 fee that schools would pay the first time they recommend an F-1 student for Optional Practical Training (OPT), plus $30,000 for each later OPT period, including the STEM OPT extension. It is a proposal for now, not a final rule. No new OPT fee applies today, and public comments are open until November 7, 2026.

DHS published the proposed rule in the Federal Register on October 8, 2026, under Docket No. ICEB-2026-0100. If it is finalized as written, a Designated School Official (DSO) could not recommend a student for OPT in SEVIS until the school pays, and USCIS would not approve the OPT work permit without that payment.

Below, we break down what the proposal says, who would actually pay, and how it affects STEM OPT and CPT. We also cover how EduConnect helps F-1 students plan their studies and work experience while the rule is still being decided.

What does the proposed OPT fee rule say?

The proposal puts a new fee on the school each time it recommends a student for OPT. It does not replace the student's existing Form I-765 application, which stays in place.

Under the proposed rule:

  • A school pays $70,000 the first time it recommends a student for any type of OPT, whether pre-completion, post-completion, or STEM OPT.
  • A school pays $30,000 for each later OPT recommendation for that same student, including the 24-month STEM OPT extension.
  • A student who uses pre-completion OPT and then post-completion OPT would cost the school $100,000 in total.
  • Changing employers within an approved OPT period would not trigger another fee, since the fee is tied to the school's recommendation and not to an employer.
  • DHS is also asking for public input on a stricter version that would charge the $70,000 again each time a student starts OPT at a higher degree level.

DHS says the fee is a response to fraud in the program and a way to protect American workers. The agency estimates about 194,000 OPT participants and 95,000 STEM OPT participants in 2024, and projects $8.4 billion to $16.5 billion in fees per year. The proposal also includes a refund process for schools whose students are not approved for OPT.

Who pays the $70,000 OPT fee?

The school pays DHS directly, but the rule leaves it up to each school to decide where the money comes from. According to the proposal, schools may pass the cost on to F-1 students, to all students, or to employers.

That means what a student actually pays would depend on the school's own policy. DHS itself acknowledges that the fee could reduce OPT participation because some schools may decline to pay it, and it expects schools to steer students toward post-completion OPT only so they pay the fee once.

If you are comparing programs right now, ask each school how it plans to handle OPT recommendations if the rule becomes final. A clear written answer is worth more than an assumption.

When could the OPT fee take effect?

Not yet. No new OPT fee is in effect today, and current OPT and STEM OPT procedures still apply.

The public comment period on the rule closes November 7, 2026. Some early coverage cited a 60-day window, but the 30-day period is the one that applies to the rule itself; a separate 60-day window covers only the information-collection portion. After comments close, DHS has to review them before it can publish a final rule, and the final version may look different from the proposal.

As proposed, a final rule would take effect 60 days after it is published. Any OPT recommendation a DSO issues before that effective date would not be subject to the new fee, and students already on approved OPT would not owe anything for their current practical training period.

The proposal could also face lawsuits from higher education groups or business associations, which could delay it. A federal court already kept DHS's duration of status rule from taking effect in September.

Anyone can submit a comment at regulations.gov under Docket No. ICEB-2026-0100. Comments that explain your specific situation, such as your field of study and career plans, tend to carry more weight than form letters.

What does the OPT fee mean for STEM OPT?

STEM students would feel this rule the most. Under the proposal, a student who completes initial OPT and then receives the 24-month STEM extension would cost the school $100,000.

There is also a timing catch. The $30,000 fee is meant to apply only where the school already paid the $70,000, so some legal analysts read the proposal to mean that a student whose first OPT was recommended before the effective date, and who requests a STEM extension after it, could trigger the full $70,000. The final rule may clarify this.

If you are on OPT now and a STEM extension is coming up, write down your dates and talk with your DSO early. Employers who rely on STEM OPT hires are watching this closely too, so it is worth keeping your manager in the loop. Related: H-1B Alternatives for International Students

Does the $70,000 OPT fee apply to CPT?

 

No, not as proposed. The rule covers OPT only. It does not create a fee for Curricular Practical Training (CPT) and does not change the CPT regulations.

CPT is still not a simple substitute for OPT. CPT has to be an integral part of your program's curriculum, and your school's DSO authorizes it for a specific employer and time period. In August 2026, SEVP sent two broadcast messages (August 12 and August 24) that tightened how schools apply that standard, so program design matters more than ever.

The 12-month rule also still applies. If you use 12 months or more of full-time CPT, you lose OPT eligibility at that degree level. Part-time CPT does not count toward that limit. With OPT potentially getting more expensive, the trade-off between full-time CPT now and OPT later deserves a fresh conversation with your DSO.

What should F-1 students do right now?

Stay calm and plan ahead. Nothing about OPT changed on October 8, and a final rule is at least weeks away.

  1. If you are on OPT now, confirm your end date and note whether a STEM extension is in your future.
  2. If you plan to apply for post-completion OPT soon, ask your DSO how the filing window lines up with a possible effective date. You can generally file up to 90 days before your program end date.
  3. If you are choosing a graduate program, ask how practical training is built into the curriculum and how the school would handle OPT recommendations under a final rule.
  4. If OPT matters to your plans, submit a comment at regulations.gov before November 7, 2026.
  5. For questions about your own case, speak with your DSO or a licensed immigration attorney before making any change to your status or employment.

Frequently asked questions about the OPT fee

Is the $70,000 OPT fee in effect now?

No. The fee is part of a proposed rule published October 8, 2026, and no new OPT fee applies today. As proposed, it would take effect 60 days after DHS publishes a final rule.

Do F-1 students pay the OPT fee themselves?

The school pays the fee to DHS. The proposal allows schools to pass the cost on to F-1 students, to all students, or to employers, so the impact on you depends on your school's policy.

How much is the STEM OPT extension fee under the proposal?

The proposal sets a $30,000 fee for each OPT recommendation after the first, including the 24-month STEM OPT extension. A student who uses initial OPT and then the STEM extension would cost the school $100,000 in total.

Does the OPT fee apply to Day 1 CPT?

No. The proposed rule covers OPT only and does not create a fee for CPT, including CPT that starts in a student's first term. CPT still has to be an integral part of the curriculum and authorized by your school's DSO.

Will using CPT affect my OPT eligibility?

It can. Using 12 months or more of full-time CPT ends your OPT eligibility at that degree level, while part-time CPT does not count toward that limit.

Does changing employers on OPT trigger a new fee?

No. Under the proposal, changing employers within an approved OPT period does not trigger another fee because the fee is tied to the school's recommendation.

When is the deadline to comment on the OPT fee rule?

The public comment period closes November 7, 2026. You can submit a comment at regulations.gov under Docket No. ICEB-2026-0100.
